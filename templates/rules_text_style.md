# Rules Text Style Guide (2024 / SRD 5.2.1)

## Preamble

This guide governs the RULES text of all homebrew content in The Guardians of Elaria: path reward documents, custom magic items (the relics and their upgrades), custom subclass features (Kensei by analogy, the Warden constellation), and any feat-shaped rewards. It does not govern narrative prose, session chronicles, or lore.

**The benchmark is SRD 5.2.1** (the 2024 revised rules). Every convention below was mined from the SRD itself, with a verbatim exemplar and page number. When you are unsure how to phrase a mechanic, find the nearest SRD analogue and copy its skeleton.

**Unearthed Arcana is NOT a style source.** UA text is draft-quality: it predates the 2024 vocabulary lockdown and freely mixes 2014 idioms ("ki points", "use your reaction", "1st-level"). Never imitate it.

**The campaign's own voice lives elsewhere.** Warmth, humor, and family flavor belong in flavor paragraphs, gold quotes, and lore boxes. The rules text underneath follows these conventions exactly, so that a feature reads like it came out of the 2024 Player's Handbook. The contrast is the point: warm frame, precise machinery.

A note on quotations: exemplars below are verbatim from the SRD, including the SRD's own em dashes and curly apostrophes where they occur. Our house style forbids em dashes (see House Deviations), so imitate the structure and vocabulary of these quotes, never their em-dash punctuation.

## 1. Core mechanics

**D20 Test is a capitalized proper term; its three kinds stay lowercase in prose.**

> These rolls are called D20 Tests, and they come in three kinds: ability checks, saving throws, and attack rolls. (p. 6)

**Success on any roll-vs-target is phrased "equals or exceeds".**

> If the total of the d20 and its modifiers equals or exceeds the target number, the D20 Test succeeds. Otherwise, it fails. (p. 6)

**Ability checks are written as Ability (Skill): capitalized ability, capitalized skill in parentheses, lowercase "check". Alternatives join with "or" inside one set of parentheses.**

> if a character tries to climb a cliff, the GM might ask for a Strength (Athletics) check (p. 9)

> if a rule allows you to make a Charisma (Deception or Persuasion) check, you add your Proficiency Bonus if you're proficient in either skill (p. 8)

**Check-gated tasks in prose use "a successful DC X [Ability] (Skill) check".**

> someone takes an action to administer first aid to it, making a successful DC 10 Wisdom (Medicine) check. (p. 17)

**Advantage and Disadvantage are always capitalized nouns. You "have Advantage/Disadvantage on" a roll; effects "grant Advantage" or "impose Disadvantage". They never stack, and one of each cancels.**

> Traveling at a Fast pace imposes Disadvantage on a traveler's Wisdom (Perception or Survival) and Dexterity (Stealth) checks. (p. 12)

> If circumstances cause a roll to have both Advantage and Disadvantage, the roll has neither of them, and you roll one d20. (p. 8)

**Damage types are capitalized wherever they appear; damage is "takes X [Type] damage". Resistance, Vulnerability, and Immunity are capitalized, take "to [Type] damage", and halving rounds down.**

> Each instance of damage has a type, like Fire or Slashing. (p. 16)

> If you have Resistance to a damage type, damage of that type is halved against you (round down). (p. 17)

**Hit Points, Hit Point maximum, and Temporary Hit Points are always capitalized. Creatures "drop to 0 Hit Points"; spells "grant" Temporary Hit Points, which are lost first and never stack.**

> if a spell grants you 12 Temporary Hit Points when you already have 10, you can have 12 or 10, not 22. (p. 18)

> If you have Temporary Hit Points and take damage, those points are lost first, and any leftover damage carries over to your Hit Points. (p. 18)

**Bonus Action and Reaction are capitalized; lowercase "action" is generic. Named actions are proper nouns followed by lowercase "action": the Attack action, the Dodge action, the Disengage action, the Utilize action, a Magic action.**

> You can take only one Bonus Action on your turn, so you must choose which Bonus Action to use if you have more than one available. (p. 10)

> You can avoid provoking an Opportunity Attack by taking the Disengage action. (p. 15)

**A Reaction self-limits until the start of your next turn.**

> When you take a Reaction, you can't take another one until the start of your next turn. (p. 10)

**Speed is capitalized (Climb Speed, Fly Speed, Swim Speed); movement is "a distance up to your Speed"; Speed 0 uses the numeral. Difficult Terrain is capitalized with per-foot costs.**

> On your turn, you can move a distance up to your Speed and take one action. (p. 13)

> Every foot of movement in Difficult Terrain costs 1 extra foot, even if multiple things in a space count as Difficult Terrain. (p. 14)

**Reach is "a 5-foot reach" / "within 5 feet" in prose and "reach 5 ft." in stat blocks. Two-number ranges are normal/long.**

> Your attack roll has Disadvantage when your target is beyond normal range, and you can't attack a target beyond long range. (p. 15)

**Short Rest and Long Rest are capitalized, with fixed durations attached.**

> Any creature can take hour-long Short Rests in the midst of a day and an 8-hour Long Rest to end it. (p. 16)

**Per-turn limits use the closing sentence "You can use this benefit only once per turn." Expendable resources use "expend"; forced rerolls state "you must use the new roll". Rounding is down, with "(round down)" at the point of use.**

> If you have Heroic Inspiration, you can expend it to reroll any die immediately after rolling it, and you must use the new roll. (p. 8)

> Doing so costs an amount of movement equal to half your Speed (round down). (p. 15)

**Dice notation is lowercase d with no space (1d20, 2d4, 4d6); dice math inside parentheses uses spaced plus signs (1d6 + 4); a natural 20 gets quotes on first use, and Critical Hit is capitalized.**

> If you roll a 20 on the d20 (called a "natural 20") for an attack roll, the attack hits regardless of any modifiers or the target's AC. This is called a Critical Hit (p. 7)

**Numerals for all game quantities** ("Speed is 0", "0 Hit Points", "within 5 feet"). Spell out "feet" in prose; abbreviate "ft." only inside stat-block attack and save lines.

## 2. Attack rolls and saves

The single most important structural split in 2024 text: prose and stat blocks phrase the same mechanic differently. Know which mode you are writing in.

**In running prose, saves use "succeed on a DC X [Ability] saving throw or [consequence]": DC first, lowercase "saving throw".**

> If an effect is about to move your mount against its will while you're on it, you must succeed on a DC 10 Dexterity saving throw or fall off, landing with the Prone condition (see "Rules Glossary") in an unoccupied space within 5 feet of the mount. (p. 16)

**In stat blocks, saves invert to a bolded lead "[Ability] Saving Throw:" (capitalized) followed by "DC X," then the targets, then terse "Failure:" and "Success:" result lines. "Success: Half damage." is the standard half-damage form.**

> Acid Spray (Recharge 6). Dexterity Saving Throw: DC 12, each creature in a 30-foot-long, 5-foot-wide Line. Failure: 14 (4d6) Acid damage. Success: Half damage. (p. 259)

**Stat-block weapon attacks use "[Name]. Melee Attack Roll: +X, reach 5 ft. Hit: ..." or "Ranged Attack Roll: +X, range 80/320 ft. Hit: ...", with average damage first and dice in parentheses.**

> Shortsword. Melee Attack Roll: +7, reach 5 ft. Hit: 7 (1d6 + 4) Piercing damage plus 17 (5d6) Poison damage, and the target has the Poisoned condition until the start of the assassin's next turn. (p. 260)

**In player-facing feature and spell text, saves against your own power use "against your spell save DC" and follow the compressed damage formula: "taking XdY damage on a failed save or half as much damage on a successful one".**

> Each creature of your choice in the Sphere must make a Constitution saving throw against your spell save DC, taking 2d6 Necrotic damage on a failed save or half as much damage on a successful one. (p. 46, Land's Aid)

**Paired save outcomes in feature text are "On a failed save, ..." then "On a successful save, ...".**

> On a failed save, the target has the Stunned condition until the start of your next turn. On a successful save, the target's Speed is halved until the start of your next turn, and the next attack roll made against the target before then has Advantage. (p. 51, Stunning Strike)

**Save-or-effect riders use "must succeed on a [Ability] saving throw or [effect]".**

> Push. The target must succeed on a Strength saving throw or be pushed up to 15 feet away from you. (p. 52, Open Hand Technique)

**Repeatable save-ending effects use the fixed sentence:**

> The creature repeats the save at the end of each of its turns, ending the effect on itself on a success. (p. 223, Gem of Brightness)

## 3. Conditions

**Conditions are capitalized and applied with the formula "has the X condition", never as a bare adjective ("is stunned", "becomes prone" are forbidden). Prose usually adds a glossary pointer, and a duration clause follows when temporary.**

> If you somehow end a turn in a space with another creature, you have the Prone condition (see "Rules Glossary") unless you are Tiny or are of a larger size than the other creature. (p. 14)

> the target has the Stunned condition until the start of your next turn (p. 51)

**Immunity to a condition is "immune to the X condition".**

> You are immune to the Poisoned condition (p. 46, Nature's Ward)

**Glossary condition entries are titled "Name [Condition]", open with the fixed sentence "While you have the X condition, you experience the following effects.", then bolded run-in sub-effects with two-to-three-word headers.**

> Grappled [Condition]
> While you have the Grappled condition, you experience the following effects.
> Speed 0. Your Speed is 0 and can't increase.
> Attacks Affected. You have Disadvantage on attack rolls against any target other than the grappler.
> Movable. The grappler can drag or carry you when it moves, but every foot of movement costs it 1 extra foot unless you are Tiny or two or more sizes smaller than it. (p. 182)

**Harsher conditions reference Incapacitated as a sub-effect rather than restating it.**

> Stunned [Condition] ... Incapacitated. You have the Incapacitated condition. (p. 189)

**Conditions never stack (Exhaustion excepted).**

> A condition doesn't stack with itself; a recipient either has a condition or doesn't. The Exhaustion condition is an exception to that rule. (p. 179)

**Standing from Prone uses the exact half-Speed phrasing.**

> Restricted Movement. Your only movement options are to crawl or to spend an amount of movement equal to half your Speed (round down) to right yourself and thereby end the condition. If your Speed is 0, you can't right yourself. (p. 186)

**Grapple escape is standardized: action, Strength (Athletics) or Dexterity (Acrobatics), against the escape DC. Free release is "(no action required)".**

> A Grappled creature can use its action to make a Strength (Athletics) or Dexterity (Acrobatics) check against the grapple's escape DC, ending the condition on itself on a success. (p. 182)

## 4. Monk vocabulary (Stabby)

The 2024 renames are mandatory. Never write "ki", "ki points", "Deflect Missiles", or "use your reaction".

**The resource is Focus Points; spending is always "expend 1 Focus Point to ..." (or "expend N Focus Points").**

> You can expend 1 Focus Point to make two Unarmed Strikes as a Bonus Action. (p. 50, Flurry of Blows)

**The monk save DC formula is worded exactly "8 plus your Wisdom modifier and Proficiency Bonus".**

> Some features that use Focus Points require your target to make a saving throw. The save DC equals 8 plus your Wisdom modifier and Proficiency Bonus. (p. 50)

**Focus Points return on any rest.**

> When you expend a Focus Point, it is unavailable until you finish a Short or Long Rest, at the end of which you regain all your expended points. (p. 50)

**Feature headers are "Level N: Feature Name". Subclasses are headed "[Class] Subclass: [Name]", then a short tagline, then flavor, then "Level N:" features (Monk subclass levels are 3, 6, 11, 17). Kensei is not in the SRD; style Stabby's Kensei material by analogy to Warrior of the Open Hand.**

> Monk Subclass: Warrior of the Open Hand
> Master Unarmed Combat Techniques
> Warriors of the Open Hand are masters of unarmed combat. (p. 52)

**Sub-options inside a feature are bold run-in headers ending with a period, followed immediately by the rules sentence. Two-tier options join with "Alternatively".**

> Patient Defense. You can take the Disengage action as a Bonus Action. Alternatively, you can expend 1 Focus Point to take both the Disengage and the Dodge actions as a Bonus Action. (pp. 50-51)

(Note: this SRD text is exactly house rule 2: the free tier is Disengage, and Dodge only arrives on the paid tier.)

**Stunning Strike opens with the once-per-turn limiter, and "stunning strike" is lowercase as the thing attempted.**

> Once per turn when you hit a creature with a Monk weapon or an Unarmed Strike, you can expend 1 Focus Point to attempt a stunning strike. (p. 51)

**Deflect Attacks (the 2024 name) is a Reaction with the reduction formula "1d10 plus your Dexterity modifier and Monk level"; the redirect costs 1 Focus Point and reuses the Dexterity-save skeleton.**

> When an attack roll hits you and its damage includes Bludgeoning, Piercing, or Slashing damage, you can take a Reaction to reduce the attack's total damage against you. The reduction equals 1d10 plus your Dexterity modifier and Monk level. (p. 51)

**Level-scaled amounts are multipliers in prose, not formulas: "five times your Monk level", "two rolls of your Martial Arts die". The die is the "Martial Arts die" and scales "as shown in the Martial Arts column of the Monk Features table".**

> You can take a Reaction when you fall to reduce any damage you take from the fall by an amount equal to five times your Monk level. (p. 51, Slow Fall)

**Limited uses tie to an ability modifier with "(minimum of once)" and the standard recovery sentence.**

> You can use this feature a number of times equal to your Wisdom modifier (minimum of once), and you regain all expended uses when you finish a Long Rest. (p. 52, Wholeness of Body)

**Free actions are flagged "(no action required)"; big saves compress to the "half as much damage" form.**

> the target must make a Constitution saving throw, taking 10d12 Force damage on a failed save or half as much damage on a successful one. (p. 52, Quivering Palm)

Reference stats at the party's level 5: PB +3, Martial Arts 1d8, 5 Focus Points, +10 ft. Unarmored Movement.

## 5. Druid vocabulary (Ursa)

**Feature headers are "Level N: Feature Name"; Druid subclass feature levels are 3, 6, 10, 14. Circle of Stars is not in the SRD; style the Warden constellation and other custom material by analogy to Circle of the Land.**

> Level 3: Circle of the Land Spells ... Level 6: Natural Recovery ... Level 10: Nature's Ward ... Level 14: Nature's Sanctuary (p. 46)

**Always-prepared spell grants say "you always have the X spell prepared", with the explicit carve-out that they do not count against prepared-spell limits.**

> you always have the Speak with Animals spell prepared. (p. 42, Druidic)

> If another Druid feature gives you spells that you always have prepared, those spells don't count against the number of spells you can prepare with this feature, but those spells otherwise count as Druid spells for you. (p. 42)

**Action costs open the sentence: "As a Bonus Action, ..." or "As a Magic action, ...".**

> As a Magic action, you can expend a use of your Wild Shape and choose a point within 60 feet of yourself. (p. 46, Land's Aid)

**Limited-use resources use the fixed recharge sentence; once-per-rest limits use "Once you use this feature/benefit, you can't do so again until you finish a Long Rest."**

> Number of Uses. You can use Wild Shape twice. You regain one expended use when you finish a Short Rest, and you regain all expended uses when you finish a Long Rest. (p. 42)

**Actionless resource spends carry "(no action required)".**

> Once on each of your turns, if you have no uses of Wild Shape left, you can give yourself one use by expending a spell slot (no action required). (p. 43, Wild Resurgence)

**Damage scaling is a single sentence naming the levels with new totals in parentheses.**

> The damage and healing increase by 1d6 when you reach Druid levels 10 (3d6) and 14 (4d6). (p. 46)

**Spell levels are "level 1", "level 1+", "level 6+" (never "1st-level"); class levels are "Druid levels 4 and 10", "half your Druid level".**

> Prepared Spells of Level 1+. You prepare the list of level 1+ spells that are available for you to cast with this feature. (p. 42)

**Multi-part features break into bold run-in sub-paragraphs ending in a period; spellcasting ability and focus each get a one-sentence run-in line.**

> Spellcasting Ability. Wisdom is your spellcasting ability for your Druid spells. (p. 42)

**Casting a spell through a feature is "expend a spell slot or a use of [resource] to cast the [Spell] spell", with component waivers inline.**

> As a Magic action, you can expend a spell slot or a use of Wild Shape to cast the Find Familiar spell without Material components. (p. 43, Wild Companion)

**Concentration is a capitalized noun; in spell blocks the duration line reads "Duration: Concentration, up to 10 minutes". Effect durations in feature text use "for 1 minute or until [condition] or die".**

> They last there for 1 minute or until you have the Incapacitated condition or die. (p. 46, Nature's Sanctuary)

**Spell blocks (for any custom spell or spell-like write-up) open with name, then "Level N School (Class, Class)" or "School Cantrip (Class list)", then four labeled lines in fixed order: Casting Time, Range, Components, Duration. Casting times are bare action nouns ("Casting Time: Action", "Casting Time: Bonus Action"), never "1 action". Costed materials use "worth N+ GP, which the spell consumes". Upcasting is a closing bold run-in "Using a Higher-Level Spell Slot." paragraph; cantrip scaling is "Cantrip Upgrade." with levels 5/11/17 and cumulative dice in parentheses.**

> Misty Step
> Level 2 Conjuration (Sorcerer, Warlock, Wizard)
> Casting Time: Bonus Action
> Range: Self
> Components: V
> Duration: Instantaneous
> Briefly surrounded by silvery mist, you teleport up to 30 feet to an unoccupied space you can see. (p. 150)

> Cantrip Upgrade. The damage increases by 1d6 when you reach levels 5 (2d6), 11 (3d6), and 17 (4d6). (p. 107, Acid Splash)

## 6. Feats structure (path rewards shaped as feats)

**Header: feat name, then a category line alone beneath it ("Origin Feat", "General Feat", "Epic Boon Feat"). Prerequisites go in parentheses on that line with the singular label "Prerequisite:", level first, in the form "Level N+"; ability scores as "Ability 13+"; class features as "[Name] Feature".**

> General Feat (Prerequisite: Level 4+, Strength or Dexterity 13+) (p. 87, Grappler)

> Fighting Style Feat (Prerequisite: Fighting Style Feature) (p. 87, Archery)

**Multi-benefit feats open with the exact stock sentence "You gain the following benefits." and contain NO flavor text. Each sub-benefit is a bold run-in header (short evocative noun phrase of one to three words, ending in a period) followed immediately by rules text.**

> Alert
> Origin Feat
> You gain the following benefits.
> Initiative Proficiency. When you roll Initiative, you can add your Proficiency Bonus to the roll.
> Initiative Swap. Immediately after you roll Initiative, you can swap your Initiative with the Initiative of one willing ally in the same combat. You can't make this swap if you or the ally has the Incapacitated condition. (p. 87)

**Single-benefit feats skip the stock line and headers entirely; one plain paragraph, optionally opened by a one-sentence flavor line. This is the ONLY place flavor appears inside SRD feat text.**

> Savage Attacker
> Origin Feat
> You've trained to deal particularly damaging strikes. Once per turn when you hit a target with a weapon, you can roll the weapon's damage dice twice and use either roll against the target. (p. 87)

**An Ability Score Increase is always a sub-benefit titled exactly "Ability Score Increase", always FIRST, phrased "Increase your X or Y score by 1, to a maximum of 20." (30 for Epic Boons).**

> Ability Score Increase. Increase your Strength or Dexterity score by 1, to a maximum of 20. (p. 87, Grappler)

**"Repeatable." is a named subsection placed LAST, with the stock sentence plus any restriction after ", but". Feats without it are once-only by rule.**

> Repeatable. You can take this feat more than once, but you must choose a different spell list each time. (p. 87, Magic Initiate)

**Recharge limits use "Once you use this benefit, you can't use it again until [trigger]." Flat bonuses are "a +N bonus to [roll/statistic]". Spellcasting grants use the stock formulas.**

> You always have that spell prepared. You can cast it once without a spell slot, and you regain the ability to cast it in that way when you finish a Long Rest. You can also cast the spell using any spell slots you have. (p. 87, Magic Initiate)

## 7. Magic item format (the relics and their upgrades)

**Header: item name on its own line, then an italic type line "Category (Subtype), Rarity (Requires Attunement ...)". Weapons name the eligible base weapon(s); restricted attunement is "Requires Attunement by a [Class]" or "by a Spellcaster".**

> Sun Blade
> Weapon (Longsword), Rare (Requires Attunement) (p. 248)

> Staff, Very Rare (Requires Attunement by a Druid, Sorcerer, Warlock, or Wizard) (p. 245, Staff of Fire)

**Charged items open "This [item] has N charges." followed by a "While holding it" clause; spending is always "expend 1 charge" (never "use" or "spend"). Casting from a charged item is "expend 1 charge to cast [Spell] (save DC N) from it"; staffs instead say "using your spell save DC".**

> This wand has 7 charges. While holding it, you can expend 1 charge to cast Web (save DC 13) from it. (p. 251, Wand of Web)

**Recharge is a bolded rider property "Regaining Charges." placed last, with the fixed formula and last-charge destruction risk. Minor items (3 charges, no risk) fold the recharge into the opening sentence.**

> Regaining Charges. The wand regains 1d6 + 1 expended charges daily at dawn. If you expend the wand's last charge, roll 1d20. On a 1, the wand crumbles into ashes and is destroyed. (p. 251, Wand of Fireballs)

> This wand has 3 charges and regains 1d3 expended charges daily at dawn. (p. 251, Wand of Secrets)

**Named item properties are bold run-in headings in title case ending with a period. Passive benefits are scoped "While wearing/holding this [item]"; activation is "you can take a Magic action / Bonus Action / Utilize action to ...". Reaction properties state the trigger first, then "you can take a Reaction to ..." (never "use your reaction").**

> Whenever an attacker makes a ranged attack roll against a target within 5 feet of you, you can take a Reaction to become the target of the attack instead. (p. 211, Arrow-Catching Shield)

**Flat bonuses: "You gain a +N bonus to attack rolls and damage rolls made with this magic weapon." AC bonuses follow the Shield pattern. Extra on-hit damage is "the target takes an extra XdY [Type] damage".**

> You gain a +1 bonus to attack rolls and damage rolls made with this magic weapon. (p. 223, Giant Slayer)

> When you hit an Undead with it, that target takes an extra 1d8 Radiant damage. (p. 248, Sun Blade)

**Once-per-day properties end with the fixed sentence:**

> Once this property is used, it can't be used again until the next dawn. (p. 210, Armor of Invulnerability)

**Light emission is always "sheds Bright Light in an N-foot radius and Dim Light for an additional N feet" (both capitalized).**

> These flames shed Bright Light in a 40-foot radius and Dim Light for an additional 40 feet. (p. 223, Flame Tongue)

**Ability-score-setting items include the no-stacking sentence; spellcasting-focus grants are a bolded "Spellcasting Focus." property.**

> Your Constitution is 19 while you wear this amulet. It has no effect on you if your Constitution is 19 or higher without it. (p. 209, Amulet of Health)

> Spellcasting Focus. While holding the hat, you can use it as a Spellcasting Focus for your Wizard spells. (p. 224, Hat of Many Spells)

**Attunement mechanics: three-item cap, Short Rest to attune, nonmagical benefits only without it. Attuned creatures know charge counts.**

> You can be attuned to no more than three magic items at a time. Any attempt to attune to a fourth item fails; you must end your Attunement to an item first. (p. 102)

> A creature attuned to an item knows how many charges the item has and how many it regains. (p. 206)

Rarity price anchors (p. 206): Common 100 GP, Uncommon 400 GP, Rare 4,000 GP, Very Rare 40,000 GP, Legendary 200,000 GP, Artifact priceless. Item type guidance for the relics: Pocket Dynamo and Sash of the Swift Current are Wondrous Items; Staff of Waking Constellations is a Staff.

## 8. Areas, vision, and teleportation

**Point-origin bursts are "a N-foot-radius Sphere" (hyphenated compound, capitalized shape) that "spreads from a point within range and fills" the area. Creature- or object-centered auras are "a N-foot Emanation" (no "-radius"). Cubes give side length: "a 15-foot Cube". Stat-block lines dimension Lines fully: "a 30-foot-long, 5-foot-wide Line".**

> For the duration, magical Darkness spreads from a point within range and fills a 15-foot-radius Sphere. (p. 122, Darkness)

> An aura radiates from you in a 30-foot Emanation for the duration. (p. 111, Aura of Life)

> cause spectral trees and vines to appear in a 15-foot Cube on the ground within 120 feet of yourself (p. 46, Nature's Sanctuary)

**Magical darkness uses the exact blocking clause, and overlap dispelling is level-gated. (Relevant to Umbral Slash, whose printed sight and placement clauses are authoritative.)**

> Darkvision can't see through it, and nonmagical light can't illuminate it. ... If any of this spell's area overlaps with an area of Bright Light or Dim Light created by a spell of level 2 or lower, that other spell is dispelled. (p. 122, Darkness)

**Light levels are capitalized game terms (Bright Light, Dim Light, Darkness), chained to obscurement in the glossary: Darkness is Heavily Obscured, and Heavily Obscured imposes Blinded on the viewer.**

> An area of Darkness is Heavily Obscured. (p. 180)

> You have the Blinded condition while trying to see something in a Heavily Obscured space. (p. 182)

**Darkvision is downgraded-by-one-step vision with the gray-colors clause; Blindsight sees "without relying on physical sight" and explicitly defeats Blinded, Darkness, and Invisible; Truesight explicitly sees in magical Darkness.**

> If you have Darkvision, you can see in Dim Light within a specified range as if it were Bright Light and in Darkness within that range as if it were Dim Light. You discern colors in that Darkness only as shades of gray. (p. 180)

**Teleportation copies Misty Step exactly: "you teleport up to N feet to an unoccupied space you can see". It costs no movement, never provokes Opportunity Attacks, and blocked destinations shunt to the nearest unoccupied space.**

> Briefly surrounded by silvery mist, you teleport up to 30 feet to an unoccupied space you can see. (p. 150, Misty Step)

> This transportation doesn't expend movement unless a rule tells you otherwise, and teleportation never provokes Opportunity Attacks. ... If the destination space of your teleportation is occupied by another creature or blocked by a solid obstacle, you instead appear in the nearest unoccupied space of your choice. (p. 190)

## House deviations

Places this campaign deliberately departs from pure SRD style. These are intentional; do not "fix" them back.

**1. Kid-facing warmth in flavor text.** SRD multi-benefit feats carry no flavor at all, and SRD item descriptions are dry. Our homebrew leads with a warm flavor paragraph, a gold quote, or a lore box in the campaign's voice (young-adult prose level, adventurous, never gruesome). The boundary is hard: once the rules text begins, the SRD register takes over completely. No jokes, endearments, or narrative asides inside a rules sentence.

**2. Personal attunement scoping.** The reward set attunes to a person, not a class: the type line reads "(Requires Attunement by Lilly)", "(Requires Attunement by Stabby)", or "(Requires Attunement by Ursa)" in place of the SRD's "by a [Class]" form (an editorial convention of this campaign). The Awakened Essence Sphere attunes to Lilly.

**3. House rules 1-7 override RAW** (from `memory/house_rules.md`, which matches the list printed in the Compendium; never regress these when writing or revising rules text):

1. Ursa's Starseed +1d4 applies only to actual druid spells, NOT the Starry Form Archer luminous arrow.
2. Patient Defense (Monk) grants a free Disengage, not Dodge. (Matches SRD 2024 text; never regress to the 2014 free Dodge.)
3. Shillelagh replaces Primal Savagery on Ursa's sheet (2024 rules).
4. Goodberry: eating a berry is a Bonus Action (2024).
5. Deep gnome uses the current 2024-era printing.
6. Lilly's cannon picks its mode on EVERY activation (Forge of the Artificer rule).
7. Potent Spellcasting rides the kindled staff once per turn (Ursa, from level 7).

Document-workflow rulings (session numbering, recency-wins) live in `memory/house_rules.md` and CLAUDE.md. Rulings from the 2026-07-13 path-reward pass are printed directly in the path documents; the printed text is authoritative.

**4. No em dashes ANYWHERE, including rules text.** The SRD itself habitually uses em dashes ("organized by category—Origin, General, Fighting Style, or Epic Boon—and alphabetized"). The family style rule outranks WotC typography: when imitating an SRD construction that uses an em dash, repunctuate with commas, colons, semicolons, or parentheses. Quoted SRD exemplars in this guide keep their original em dashes because quotes are verbatim; new text never does.

**5. "DM", not "GM".** SRD 5.2.1 rules text says "GM". This is a family campaign with a DM (Dad); homebrew text that needs the referee says "DM".

## Compliance checklist

Run any homebrew rules block against these before it ships. Every answer should be yes.

1. Zero em dashes anywhere in the block, including inside rules sentences (outside verbatim SRD quotes)?
2. Every condition applied with "has/have the X condition" (capitalized), never a bare adjective like "is stunned" or "knocked prone"?
3. Every damage instance in the form "takes XdY [Type] damage" with the damage type capitalized?
4. Game terms capitalized throughout: Advantage, Disadvantage, Bonus Action, Reaction, Speed, Hit Points, Temporary Hit Points, Short Rest, Long Rest, Bright Light, Dim Light, Resistance, Concentration?
5. Action costs phrased 2024-style ("As a Bonus Action, ...", "take a Magic action", "take a Reaction to ..."), with no 2014 idioms ("use your reaction", "as an action", "1st-level", "ki")?
6. Prose saves in the form "must succeed on a DC X [Ability] saving throw or [consequence]", and half-damage saves in the form "taking XdY damage on a failed save or half as much damage on a successful one"?
7. Ability checks written as "[Ability] ([Skill]) check", with DCs stated as "DC X" before the ability?
8. Limited-use features carrying an exact recharge sentence ("Once you use this feature, you can't use it again until you finish a Long Rest." or the Short/Long Rest formula), and actionless spends flagged "(no action required)"?
9. Per-turn limits closed with "You can use this benefit only once per turn." or opened with "Once per turn when you ..."?
10. Feature headers in "Level N: Feature Name" form, and sub-options as bold run-in headers ending with a period?
11. Magic items headed with an italic type line "Category (Subtype), Rarity (Requires Attunement by Lilly/Stabby/Ursa)", charges spent with "expend", and "Regaining Charges." as the last property where applicable?
12. Feat-shaped rewards using the exact stock lines: "You gain the following benefits.", "Prerequisite: Level N+", Ability Score Increase first, "Repeatable." last (or absent)?
13. Areas phrased as "N-foot-radius Sphere", "N-foot Emanation", or "N-foot Cube", and teleports as "teleport up to N feet to an unoccupied space you can see"?
14. No house rule (1-7) contradicted, and no regression of a locked fix (Patient Defense free Disengage, Starseed scope, Shillelagh swap, Bonus Action Goodberry)?
15. All flavor, warmth, and campaign voice confined to flavor paragraphs, gold quotes, and lore boxes, with the rules text underneath indistinguishable in register from SRD 5.2.1?
