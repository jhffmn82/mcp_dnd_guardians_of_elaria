# Session 8, Part Two: Underroot

*The rift run. Board: `assets/session_08/underroot_board.png`.*

---

## How the board works

The party moves as **one token**. On their turn a player rolls **1d6** and moves that many spaces, and whoever is holding the token rotates each roll so every kid gets to move it.

They land on a colour and it resolves. **Read the die they just moved with: EVEN or ODD picks which of the two outcomes happens.** No tables to look up, no second roll.

| Dot | Colour | Even | Odd |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Move** | slate blue | Nothing. Keep going. | Nothing. Keep going. |
| **Encounter** | garnet | **Thumpaw** | **The Gleamoth** |
| **Boon** | moss | **The Deepwater Spring** | **The Shortcut** |
| **Hazard** | ochre | **Sinkloam** | **The Ceiling Lets Go** |
| **Wild** | amethyst, ! | **The Reef Answers** | **The Guardian's Dream** |

All eight are written out in *The Road Between*, below.

**The four numbered locations are not dots.** They are fixed story stops: the token stops at each one regardless of movement remaining, the beat plays, and movement resumes next turn. **The Rift Gate is the arrival and has no fight.**

### One rule above all the others: they came here to fight

The kids come to this table to fight things. Objectives, levers and clever plays exist to make the fighting *better*, and they must never be an off-ramp that skips it. Nothing in this session can be talked down, waited out, or avoided. The levers below all do the same thing: they turn a grinding fight into a satisfying one, and finding them is the reward.

### The creature roster

Ten creatures, and **every scripted fight uses two types** so no encounter is a single wall of the same thing.

| Fight | Bruiser | Pressure |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Mosslight Landing | **Rotblooms** (re-bloom until burned) | **Mossmites** (fast, weak, swarm) |
| 2. Chime Reef | **Chimestones** (armoured, stun on thunder) | **Shardwings** (flying, dive, ranged) |
| 3. Glassed Gallery | **Glass Weeper** (regenerates) | **Cinderolls** (roll in and detonate) |
| 4. Groudon's Hollow | **GROUDON** (the spike) | **Glasslings** (spawned BY the spike) |

**Random encounters only:** **Thumpaw** and the **Gleamoth swarm**. These two never appear at a scripted fight and are the only creatures the party meets purely by luck, which makes a garnet dot feel different from a story beat instead of feeling like filler.

**Glasslings matter mechanically.** The spike spawns one at the start of each of Groudon's turns. That is the pressure that stops the party from ignoring the spike and plinking Groudon: the longer the spike lives, the more little glass things are chewing on them. Break it and every remaining Glassling collapses instantly, which is a fantastic moment.

### Four fights, and where they fall

The shape of the evening is fixed, and the dots are pacing between the fixed points:

| # | Where | Who | Difficulty | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Mosslight Landing | 6 Rotblooms + 6 Mossmites | warm-up, but it runs long | Teaches the rule: corruption re-blooms unless Stabby burns it. |
| **2** | Chime Reef | 3 Chimestones + 3 Shardwings | **hard**, or easy if they use thunder | The fight with a lever in it. |
| | | **SHORT REST** | | Immediately after the reef. Call it out loud: Hit Dice spent, Stabby's Focus back, Ursa's Wild Shape back, Lilly's Aether Ward back. |
| **3** | The Glassed Gallery | Glass Weeper + 4 Cinderolls | **hardest before the boss** | Stabby's proving ground. |
| **4** | Groudon's Hollow | Groudon + Glasslings | boss, but see The Spike | The rescue. |

> **A note on the numbers.** Fights 2 and 3 are deliberately above a "deadly" budget on paper, and both have a lever that collapses them: **thunder stuns a Chimestone and doubles all damage against it**, and **tending the roots strips the Weeper's regeneration**. A party that finds the lever wins comfortably. A party that ignores it is in a real fight. That gap is the fun, so do not soften either one before the players have had a chance to be clever.

A garnet Encounter dot between these is **small pressure**, two or three weak things that cost a little resource and no more. Do not let a dot roll become a fifth big fight; the four scripted ones are the session.

**Pace check.** There are 22 dots between the gate and the Hollow. If the evening is running long, hand-wave the dots between locations 3 and 4 and go straight to Groudon. The four story stops are the session; the dots are texture.

# The Descent

## 1. The Rift Gate: the first breath

> **Read aloud.** You expected a cave.
>
> What you are standing in is a root. One root, arching over you like the ceiling of a cathedral, running away into the dark in both directions further than your light reaches. The ground under your boots is warm, and soft, and it is breathing: a slow swell and settle, about once every minute, so gentle you would miss it if you were not standing still.
>
> There is no sky. There is no sun. There is light, though, a low green-gold glow coming off the moss in patches, enough to see by, and the air smells like a garden turned over after rain.
>
> Behind you the ring hangs in the air, a bright coin of Gearhaven lamplight, and Vane's shape is standing in it with his arms folded, getting smaller.

**Beats to land here:**

- **The Sphere is warm** in Lilly's hands, and it is *pulling*. She can feel it gathering. It has never done that before and it does not stop for the rest of the session.
- **Ursa cannot navigate.** Say it to his player directly. No sky, no stars, and his year of star-reading buys him nothing. What does work is what Selarin told him: read the ground. Give him advantage on Survival down here for reading root growth and soil.
- **Stabby's idol is cold.** Colder than it was in the Rift Hall. He will keep checking it all session and it will keep getting worse.
- **Ghostbloom is delighted.** She is a plant-spirit standing inside a plane made of growing things and she has never been so happy. Let her player-facing chime be joy, all session, right up until the Hollow.

**No encounter.** They get one safe scene to be amazed. Do not spend it.

## 2. Mosslight Landing: the friendly face

*Board location 1.*

> **Read aloud.** The root opens into a hollow the size of a village, and it is beautiful. Round mossy mounds swell out of the floor with little round doors of packed earth, though nobody built them and nobody lives in them: that is simply the shape moss grows in here. Threads of pale gold light drift up from the ground like slow sparks going the wrong way.
>
> Something is watching you from the top of the nearest mound. It is about the size of a badger, armoured in overlapping plates of yellow-brown hide, with small dark eyes and enormous digging claws, and it is absolutely rigid with the effort of not being seen.

**This is the Sandshrew**, and this is the only time the party meets it in the flesh before the homecoming. **It cannot be caught, tamed, or bagged.** It watches them. If they are kind or feed it, it follows at a distance for the rest of the run and is present at the end. If they are aggressive, it vanishes and joins them at the homecoming anyway, which will feel like a gift they did not earn.

**Ursa's Mark of Handling works on it** and should be the moment his player realises the plane's wildlife is not the enemy.

### Encounter 1: the Rotblooms

The pretty part of Underroot has a wound in it. On the far mounds the moss is **grey and wet and wrong**, and standing in it, swaying, are six little flower-creatures.

They were lovely a month ago. You can still see it: round soft bodies, stubby arms, big round flower-caps that used to be gold. The caps are grey now, and slack, and they leak. When they notice the party they all turn at once, and their faces are still smiling, because their faces cannot do anything else.

**5 Rotblooms and 5 Mossmites** (statblocks below). The Mossmites boil out of the moss the moment the first Rotbloom is struck: tiny, fast, six-legged, one enormous eye each, individually harmless and collectively a nuisance that keeps everyone honest while the real problem re-blooms behind them.

> **Read aloud.** Ghostbloom makes a sound you have never heard her make. She knows what these are. They are almost the same as her.

- **Design intent: this fight exists to hand Stabby the session.** Rotblooms re-bloom every round and ordinary weapons cannot finish them: cut one down and it swells back up out of its own cap. **Cleansing Edge is the answer** and it is the only one the party has. Let them flail for a round, let Lilly and Ursa watch their damage undo itself, then let Stabby ignite and cut one and have it *stay* cut.
- **Six of them, and they hit in a cloud.** This is a proper scrap, not a speed bump: Spore Puff means the whole party is making saves, Puff and Ghostbloom are in the mix, and everyone gets three or four rounds of swinging.
- **Say it out loud when it lands:** he spent a year on a mountain learning to burn corruption out of the ground, and this is the first time anyone else has seen it work. His player has been carrying that backstory alone since the reading. Give him the beat.
- **The tell:** they do not attack the Sandshrew, and it does not run from them. It hides from the *party*. Corruption does not eat its own.

## 3. Chime Reef: the beautiful trap

*Board location 2.*

> **Read aloud.** The tunnel opens and you all stop at once, because the sound arrives before the sight does.
>
> Crystals. Thousands of them, growing up out of a shallow black pool in spires and fans and thickets, blue and gold and green, some of them taller than Vane's workshop. And the whole reef is *ringing*, very quietly, a slow shifting chord you feel in your back teeth. When the ground breathes, the note changes.
>
> Ghostbloom makes a sound none of you have heard her make before. Ursa, without deciding to, has taken his hand off his staff.

**Beats:**

- **The reef answers sound.** Anything loud makes the crystals ring louder, and a big enough noise brings a spire down. Reward players who work this out and use it.
- **Lilly can read the growth pattern** with an Arcana check: the crystals are growing *toward* something deeper in, the way plants grow toward light. Whatever is down there, the whole reef is leaning on it. (It is Groudon.)
- **A quiet gift.** If anyone spends a turn just listening, Elaria's presence is faintly here, in the chord. Ursa's player may notice it is the same seven-note shape as the Havenmoor bells.

### Encounter 2: the Chimestones

Four boulders in the shallows unfold legs and stand up.

They are squat and heavy and armoured in deep blue-black rock, with blunt jaws and small bright amber eyes, and jagged orange crystals growing out of their backs and shoulders like a struck chord frozen in place. They are **not corrupted**. They are the reef's immune system, and the party is loud and warm and came in through a hole.

**3 Chimestones and 3 Shardwings** (statblocks below). The Chimestones are the wall. The Shardwings are the reason nobody gets to stand still: pale crystal darts the size of hawks that dive, spit shards from sixty feet, and are almost impossible to pin down. They are also **vulnerable to thunder**, so the same lever works on the whole encounter.

- **This is a real fight and they are tough.** AC 16, they hit hard, and they resonate: every round the whole reef rings louder and everybody's ears hurt.
- **The clever play does not end the fight, it wins it.** Thunder damage makes a Chimestone ring itself apart: any thunder damage **stuns it until the end of its next turn**, and it takes **double damage** while stunned. Lilly's Boomstick blast is thunder, and so are her Thunderwave and Shatter and Ursa's Thunderwave: any of them turns one of these from a wall into a free kill. (Stabby's Nichirin is steel and force, not thunder; the reef does not ring for him.)
- **The reef itself is a weapon.** Anyone can spend an action to strike a big crystal spire (DC 13 Athletics): the note rolls out and **every Chimestone in 30 ft. must save or be stunned**. Reward the player who thinks of it. Let them bring the roof down on the last one if they earn it.
- **The trade-off that keeps it honest:** the ringing hurts the party too. Anyone who uses the spire trick takes 1d6 thunder themselves. Nobody minds.

## 4. The Glassed Gallery: the wound

*Board location 3.*

> **Read aloud.** You know something is wrong a hundred feet before you reach it, because the ground stops breathing.
>
> Ahead of you the roots are **black glass**. Not burned: glassed, smooth and shining and hard, frozen mid-growth in the middle of reaching for something. The whole gallery is silent in a way nothing else down here has been. There is no moss light. Your own lamps look small.
>
> And every surface, when you get close, is covered in fine cracks that are very slowly, very quietly, spreading.

**This is what the rift is doing to Underroot**, and the party should feel the size of it. This is not a monster's lair. It is a burn scar going outward at the speed of grass growing, and it has been going for two years.

**Beats:**

- **The idol is now painfully cold.** Stabby has to wrap it in cloth to carry it. It has never behaved like this. *(Do not explain. Door C seed.)*
- **Lilly's Sphere is drinking.** It is visibly brighter here, and it is gathering fastest where the damage is worst. She can work out that the loose essence bleeding out of this wound is exactly what her Sphere was built to collect.
- **The dead giveaway:** the cracks all radiate outward from one direction. Deeper in. Toward the Hollow.

### Encounter 3: the Glass Weeper and the Cinderolls

Something is still standing in the gallery.

It was one of the big slow root-tenders once: a long-necked creature that anchored itself in the soil and swayed, with a crown of soft fronds it used to comb the roots clean. The corruption caught it mid-stride and turned all of it to black glass, fronds and all, and it is **still trying to do its job**, sweeping shattered glass fronds through glassed roots that cannot feel it.

Around its feet, rolling in fast, are lumps of black glassy coal the size of footballs, each with one furious orange eye.

**1 Glass Weeper and 5 Cinderolls** (statblocks below), the Cinderolls arriving **two at a time** so the pressure never stops but never quite swamps them.

- **This is the big fight of the run before the boss, and it should be hard.** The Weeper hits for real, it regenerates 10 a round, and the Cinderolls keep rolling in and detonating.
- **The clever play strips its armour instead of ending the fight.** The Weeper is bound to the glassed roots it is tending. Any character who spends an action clearing or healing the roots nearby (DC 14 Nature or Medicine, or any healing spell aimed at the roots) makes the Weeper **lose its regeneration for one round** and gives everyone **advantage on attacks against it until the start of their next turn**. Two or three of those and the fight tips hard. It never ends the encounter: it makes the party feel brilliant while they finish it.
- **This is Stabby's proving ground.** The Weeper regenerates 10 a round and the party cannot out-damage it. Only radiant, force, or **Cleansing Edge** shuts that off, and Stabby is the reliable source. He is the reason this fight is winnable and everyone at the table should be able to see it.
- **The gallery itself is his too.** After the fight, if he walks the glassed roots with his breath lit, the corruption recoils from the Nichirin exactly as the blight did at home. He cannot heal the gallery, it is far too big, but he can burn a clean path through it, and the party walks out of the wound on a road he made. That image is the whole point of his year.
- **When it falls** it shatters, and inside the glass, curled up where the heart would be, is a small ordinary creature that has been dead for two years. That is the moment the session turns from an adventure into a rescue, and it is the last beat before Groudon.

## 5. Groudon's Hollow: the Guardian

*Board location 4. The boss.*

> **Read aloud.** The tunnel ends and there is no floor, only a drop into a chamber so large your light does not find the far side of it.
>
> Something down there is breathing. You have been feeling it since you arrived, the slow swell of the ground every minute or so, and now you understand what you have been standing on top of all day. It was never the plane breathing.
>
> It was **him**.
>
> He fills the hollow: a mountain with a spine, red-brown plates the size of barn doors, claws sunk deep into stone he has not moved from in a very long time. Glowing seams of magma run down his flanks like veins. And driven into his back, up near the shoulder where he cannot possibly reach it, is a **spike of black glass**, and the cracks in the world spread out from that one point.
>
> He is the reason there is dry land anywhere. And he is in agony, and he has been for two years, and he has not been able to say so.

### The fight, and what it is actually about

**Groudon is not the villain. The spike is.** The Grand Custodian rule applies exactly: *free the guardian and the cracks close.*

- **Do not let the party win by damage.** Groudon has more hit points than they can chew through and hits hard enough to drop a level-7 hero in two turns. If they simply brawl, they lose.
- **The win condition is the spike.** It is a hard target on his back: reachable by climbing (Athletics/Acrobatics), by flight (Stabby's Sash, Ursa's Starry Form), or by a called shot at range with disadvantage. It has **AC 17 and 60 hit points**, and it is **immune to everything except radiant, force, and Cleansing Edge**.
- **When the spike breaks, the fight ends instantly.** Not "he has 40 HP left." It ends.

### The Glasslings

**The spike creates one Glassling at the start of each of Groudon's turns**: a cat-sized scuttling thing of black glass that drops off his back and goes for the nearest warm body. They are weak, they explode when killed, and they never stop coming.

They exist to answer the obvious question, which is *why not just ignore the spike and plink the big guy?* Because the longer the spike lives, the more of these are chewing on the party. **When the spike breaks, every Glassling on the field collapses into sand at once**, and that is a genuinely great moment: the whole board clears in a heartbeat.

**This is the shape of the encounter:** survive a very large angry mountain long enough for somebody to get up his back and break the thing hurting him. Every character has a role and each should get their moment:

- **Stabby is the one who ends this, and the whole session has been building to it.** Speed 65 ignited, Step of the Wind to close, and the Nichirin is one of only three things in the world that can touch the spike. He climbs a mountain that is trying to shake him off and he cuts the corruption out of a god. **This is his moment. Do not let anybody else take it unless his player hands it over.** If the dice go badly, let Ursa's summon pin Groudon or Lilly's Flash of Genius rescue the climb check, so the last blow is still his.
- **Ursa** keeps everyone alive and can put a summon between Groudon and whoever is climbing. His Mark of Handling lets him *feel* that the creature is in pain, which nobody else can, and telling the others is the moment the fight becomes a rescue.
- **Lilly** shoots the spike from range and holds the party together with Aether Ward. Force damage from Puff's Wand of Magic Missiles works on the spike and never misses.

### Groudon's turns

Full statblock below. He is played as **a natural disaster with a wounded animal inside it**, not a tactician. Alternate:

1. **Continental Step:** he moves. The floor changes. Everyone makes a Dexterity save or is knocked prone and takes falling rubble damage.
2. **Magma Vent:** a line or cone of superheated stone.
3. **Claw and Slam:** if anything is in reach.
4. **Thrash (when the spike is damaged):** he screams and everything on him must hold on.

**Mercy rule for the table:** if a hero drops, Groudon does not finish them. He does not know they are there in any meaningful way. He is swatting at pain.

# The Road Between

Underroot is not empty between the landings. Eight things can happen out there, and which one you get is simply **whether the movement die came up even or odd**. No table to look up, no second roll, no pause.

---

## Thumpaw

*Garnet dot, even. Plate D1.*

> A shape unfolds out of what you took for a boulder. It is the size of a cart horse, round and shaggy with matted brown moss growing over stone-grey hide, and it has hands like shovels. It looks at you with small patient eyes and does not move, because you are standing in the tunnel it was going to use.

**1 Thumpaw.** It will not move and it is directly in the way, and when somebody inevitably prods it, it swings.

- **This is a straight brawl and that is the point.** 76 hit points, two big slams a turn, no gimmick, no puzzle. Sometimes a table just wants to hit something large until it stops, and this is that.
- It never pursues and it never finishes a downed hero. It is grumpy, not evil.
- *(If the party genuinely tries to befriend it rather than fight it, DC 15 Animal Handling with advantage from Ursa's Mark will move it aside. Do not offer this. Let them think of it.)*

## The Gleamoth

*Garnet dot, odd. Plate D2.*

> The lights come round the corner before the sound does: forty pale green sparks drifting like blown seeds. They are moths, each the size of a hand, and every one of them is glowing, and they want the warmest thing in the tunnel, which is you.

**3 Gleamoth Swarms.** Not corrupted, just hungry for heat.

- They **blind and smother** rather than damage much, and they go for whoever is carrying light.
- **Fire and thunder scatter them fast**; anything that hits an area is worth double here.
- **The cost is the Sphere.** They are drawn to it hardest, so Lilly has the swarm on her the whole fight, which makes the party come to her.

---

## The Deepwater Spring

*Moss dot, even. Plate D3.*

> A crack in the wall, and inside it a still pool of water so clear that you can see forty feet down to the pale stones at the bottom. It is very cold, and it tastes like the air after lightning.

Everyone regains **1 Hit Die** and clears **one level of exhaustion**. Ghostbloom glows brighter for an hour and is insufferable about it.

## The Shortcut

*Moss dot, odd. Plate D4.*

> Something small and yellow-brown is standing on a mound ahead of you, up on its back legs, watching. When it is sure you have seen it, it drops down and starts digging, very fast, and the tunnel it opens goes exactly the way you were trying to go.

**Advance the token 3 spaces free.** If the party was unkind to it at Mosslight Landing, it watches them take the long way round instead, and that should sting.

---

## Sinkloam

*Ochre dot, even. Plate D5.*

> The floor stops being floor. It happens without a sound: one step is packed earth and the next is warm wet loam that comes up over your boots and keeps coming.

Everyone makes a **DC 13 Strength save** or is **restrained**, spending an action to pull free. Heavy armour has disadvantage, so Lilly is the one in trouble and somebody has to go back for her.

## The Ceiling Lets Go

*Ochre dot, odd. Plate D6.*

> Far above you, something enormous rolls over in its sleep, and the ceiling of the world lets go.

**DC 14 Dexterity save**, **2d10 bludgeoning** on a fail, half on a success. The passage behind them is now blocked, which matters if anyone was planning to run.

---

## The Reef Answers

*Amethyst dot, even. Plate D7.*

> Somewhere far off, a single crystal note. Then another underneath it, and another, until the whole tunnel is ringing in a slow enormous chord you can feel in your ribs, and the moss on the walls lights up in time with it.

Everyone gains **2d8 temporary hit points** and Ghostbloom sings back, badly, at the top of her voice. **This is a good place for the short rest** if the party has not taken it yet.

## The Guardian's Dream

*Amethyst dot, odd. Plate D8.*

> For half a second all four of you see the same thing, and none of you asked for it.
>
> An empty sea under an empty sky, going on forever. And something walking through it, huge and slow and patient, and where it walks the water shoulders aside and dry land comes up behind it, steaming, new.
>
> Then it is gone and you are standing in a tunnel again.

No mechanical effect. **They have just seen Groudon remember his own job**, and they will not understand it until they are standing in front of him. Ursa may **regain one spent Omen Dream**, because something just spoke to him and he does not know what.

## In the Glassed Gallery

### Glass Weeper

# The Resolution

## When the spike breaks

> **Read aloud.** The black glass does not shatter. It comes apart like a held breath let go, all at once and quietly, into a spray of dark motes that hang in the air for a moment and then rush, all of them together, into the Sphere in Lilly's hands.
>
> And the mountain stops.
>
> Groudon goes absolutely still, and then he lowers himself down onto his belly on the stone with a sound like a hillside settling, and he lets out a breath that goes on for eleven seconds, and every root in the hollow shivers.
>
> Then he turns his head, slowly, and looks at the three of you properly for the first time.
>
> He is not angry. He is so tired.

**The Mote.** As the glass leaves him, something else comes loose: a fist-sized, dense, red-brown ember of pure Earth, the piece of this plane's heart that was caught here. It drifts to the Sphere on its own. **This is the Mote of Terra.**

**What Groudon does:** he touches one claw, very carefully, to the ground in front of Ursa. Nobody needs the Mark to understand it. Then he starts to walk, and where he walks the cracks close behind him, and the black glass in the gallery greys and crumbles into ordinary soil.

**Give Ursa this line if his player wants it:** he is the only one who can feel what the creature is feeling, and what it is feeling is *relief*.

## Antares

Ursa should be the one who notices, and it should happen after they step back through.

> **Read aloud, in Gearhaven.** You come out into lamplight and noise and Vane shouting, and it is a long few minutes before anybody looks up.
>
> Ursa does. Through the high windows of the Rift Hall, over the roofs of Gearhaven, there is a star in the east that was dying.
>
> It is not dying now. It is the deep steady red it is supposed to be, and it is the first star in living memory to come back.

**Only Ursa understands what he is looking at.** He has spent a year with his father's charts. Let his player say it out loud to the others, and let Aelwyn go very quiet, because Aelwyn has just watched a theory become a fact.

**The Staff of Waking Constellations does this**, and it is what it was made for: one new star brightens on its haft.

# The Aftermath

## The homecoming, and the two creatures

This is the canon beat and it needs room.

> **Read aloud.** The Sphere will not settle.
>
> It has been getting brighter since Underroot and now it is too bright to look at directly, warm as a stove, humming against Lilly's palms. Aelwyn takes one look and tells everybody to stand back, and for once he is not delighted, he is *nervous*, which is much worse.
>
> "It gathered more than it can hold," he says. "It only keeps one shape at a time. It is going to have to choose."

**Ghostbloom chooses first.** Nobody asks her to. She looks at Lilly, and chimes once, and steps into the light and is gone, and the Sphere goes calm.

**Play this straight and let it hurt for a second.** The kids will think they have lost her. They have not: she is the tether, she is fine, she can come back out, and Aelwyn explains that afterward. But give them three or four seconds of thinking otherwise before he does.

**Then the gathered essence takes shape**, and what steps out of the light is the small armoured digger from Mosslight Landing, blinking in the lamplight, entirely solid and entirely real.

- If they were kind to it in the Landing, it goes straight to whoever fed it.
- If they were not, it goes to Ursa, because his Mark is the warmest thing in the room, and that will sting in a way they deserve.

**Aelwyn's explanation, in kid words:** the Sphere gathers. It gathered a whole plane's worth of loose essence today, and essence gathers toward warmth and company, and so it brought somebody home.

### The rule the party just learned: SWAPPING

Teach this properly. It is the mechanic the rest of the campaign runs on and this is its first outing.

- **The Sphere holds ONE physical companion at a time.** Everyone else rides inside it, safe and aware and perfectly fine.
- **Swapping takes a short rest**, done by whoever holds the Sphere. The one outside steps in, the one they choose steps out. Nobody is hurt by it and nobody is ever lost.
- **You choose before you go somewhere, not in the middle of a fight.** That is the interesting decision. Ghostbloom heals and senses planar things; Sandshrew digs and takes hits and is made of the same stuff as the ground. Picking wrong for a plane is a real mistake and an entirely survivable one.
- **The roster grows by one per rift.** Say that out loud. Four more planes, four more companions. The kids should start arguing immediately about who they want next, and they will be right to.

**Release Sandshrew into their hands and let them do the first swap themselves that evening**, just to prove it works and get Ghostbloom back out. Let them name the arrangement.

## What each hero gets out of it

- **Lilly.** The Mote of Terra socketed into the Sphere. **Her first Elemental Discharge wakes: Tectonic Slam.** Four still sleep. Her power now grows by going out, exactly as she worked out during her year, and now she has proof.

  > **Give her player this beat at the workbench that night.** She has the Sphere open, the Mote sitting in its new socket, and she is not looking at anybody.
  >
  > "It isn't full. That's the thing. There's a whole *plane* in there and it barely moved the needle." She turns it over in her hands. "I can feel where the other four go. There are sockets, and they're empty, and when I put my hand flat on it I can almost hear what it would sound like with all five."
  >
  > "I don't think it's a battery. I think it's an instrument. And I've only ever played one note on it."

  **What that plants:** her ceiling is very high and entirely gated on rifts cleared. She has gone from one discharge to a promise of five, and she is the only person at the table who can feel the shape of the thing she is going to become.
- **Ursa.** A relit star and the first hard evidence that the damage can be *undone*. Also the beginning of a private worry: the thing that put the spike in Groudon did it on purpose, and it has a name, and he brought that name home from a shrine.
- **Stabby.** The idol warmed up the moment they came back through, and he noticed exactly when. He still has not mentioned it to anybody.

## Threads to leave hanging

1. **Who drove the spike in?** Nobody says the name at the table yet. But the party has now seen that the rifts are not weather. Somebody is doing this by hand.
2. **The idol.** Cold in Underroot, colder at the Gallery, warm at home.
3. **Four stars to go**, and two of them are still bright, which Aelwyn will realise before the next session is out means they can get there *first*.

> **The last line of the session, if you want one.** Aelwyn is still looking east at Antares, and he says, to nobody: "One."
