# Warden's Mantle: temp-HP revision (staged, not applied)

## Why

The Starlit Channeler door makes Ursa a front-liner, and it does so structurally, not
optionally. **Warden's Intercession only protects allies "within your Starlight,"** so the
reaction is dead weight from the back rank. The door forces him to stand where the hits land,
and it does that to a character with **AC 18, roughly 52 HP at level 7, and a Constitution save
of +2**.

Two separate problems come out of that, and only one of them was already solved:

- **Losing concentration.** Already handled, and handled well, by the **Dragon** constellation,
  which treats a d20 of 9 or lower as a 10 on Concentration saves. With Con +2 that is a floor
  of 12 against a DC of "10 or half the damage taken," so chip damage cannot break his spell.
  Critically, this costs him something real: choosing Dragon means giving up Archer's luminous
  arrow or Chalice's healing. No new rules text is needed here.
- **Dropping.** Not handled. Temporary hit points absorbed still trigger the Concentration save,
  so Dragon does nothing for survival, and nothing else on the door does either.

**The fix is closer to a correction than a buff.** Base 2024 Wild Shape already grants
"Temporary Hit Points equal to your Druid level" to any druid who assumes a form
(`reference/srd/04_classes_druid_fighter_monk.md`). Ursa spends the identical resource, a Wild
Shape use, to enter Starry Form and receives none of it, purely because Starry Form is a form he
does not shape into. This restores parity and adds a modest amount on top for a door whose whole
identity is holding the line.

**Amount: Wisdom modifier + Druid level (12 at level 7).** Considered and rejected: flat Druid
level (7) is exact parity but thin for a dedicated front-liner; twice Druid level (14) is double
the RAW baseline and, across three Wild Shape uses, approaches 42 temporary hit points against a
52-point pool, which nearly doubles him on a door that already carries Warden's Intercession as a
second defensive layer.

## The intended playstyle this supports

The Warden turn uses all three action types every round, which nothing else in the party does:

- **Action:** Shillelagh swing, using Wisdom.
- **Bonus Action:** move a damage aura. **Flaming Sphere is the partner spell**, since 2024 moves
  it as a Bonus Action. **Moonbeam works only if parked**, because 2024 changed moving it to a
  Magic action. **Call Lightning does not fit**, needing an Action every turn.
- **Reaction:** Warden's Intercession, eating a hit meant for someone else.
- **Passive:** Dragon holding his own concentration, and handing the same floor to every ally in
  his Starlight.

Three doors, three verbs: Beast brings friends, Reader bends fate, **Warden holds the ground**.

## The change

Insert one clause into **The Warden's Mantle**, on the trigger that is already there. Everything
else in the feat, and the Constellation Gifts, are unchanged.

> **The Warden's Mantle.** You learn the Shillelagh cantrip, and Wisdom is your spellcasting
> ability for it. When you assume your Starry Form, you cast Shillelagh through your Staff of
> Waking Constellations as part of the same Bonus Action, the spell lasts until the form ends
> rather than for 1 minute, and **you gain Temporary Hit Points equal to your Wisdom modifier
> plus your Druid level**. While your Starry Form is active, whichever constellation you chose:
> the Staff's Starlight kindles with the form (no action required) and reaches 20 feet, and each
> ally within that light gains a +1 bonus to attack rolls and saving throws; the kindled staff
> deals Radiant damage, and when you hit with it, the target takes an extra 1d6 Radiant damage
> (this extra damage increases to 2d6 at Druid level 11 and 3d6 at Druid level 17); and the
> Warden, a spear-bearing figure of starlight, stands guard in your glow.

Optional flavor line for the road's closing paragraph, if it wants one:

> The Warden does not only stand between his friends and the dark. It stands between the dark
> and the boy carrying the light.
