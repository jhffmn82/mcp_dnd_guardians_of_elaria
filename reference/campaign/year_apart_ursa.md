# Ursa Catchum: The Road East

## Part One: What Elder Selarin Would Not Give Him

The other two went home. Ursa went the other way.

He walked back into the Circle of the Eternal Stars for exactly nine days, which was long enough to do one thing, and the one thing was to ask Elder Selarin for his father's papers.

Selarin said no.

It is worth understanding why, because Selarin is not a villain and the scene plays better if the table can feel both sides of it. He raised Ursa. He was there the morning Ash Catchum walked east and did not come back, and he has spent the years since watching a boy grow up in the shape of that absence. Ash's field notes are in a locked cabinet in Selarin's own quarters, and they have been for years, and the reason is simple: they are the notes of a man who followed a thing that swallows sky-readers, and they are extremely persuasive.

"You are eight," Selarin said.

"I know how old I am," said Ursa.

They argued for nine days. On the ninth, Selarin unlocked the cabinet, because he had known from the first hour that he was going to and had been buying time to make his peace with it. He handed the whole bundle over without a word, and then he said one thing, which Ursa has repeated to nobody:

*Your father read the sky better than any of us. It did not save him. Read the ground too.*

> **Illustration slot.** Selarin at the cabinet, the bundle of notes held out, the boy's hands already reaching. Firelight, night through the window, one bright star.

### What was in the bundle

Not a map. That is the first disappointment and it is important.

Ash Catchum's notes are the working papers of a man in the middle of a thought: sky charts with dates, columns of numbers, the same constellation drawn eleven times over eleven months with one point of light getting fainter each time. Pages of shorthand nobody else could read. Sketches in the margins, some of them beautiful, most of them of things Ursa could not identify.

And running through all of it, underlined twice and never explained, one word: **Betelgeuse.**

The first star to go out. The one his father was chasing.

The notes stop in the middle of a page. Not at the end of a thought. In the middle of one.

## Part Two: The Skyless Country

He went east on foot and alone.

That is the part the other two did not have and it should sit heavy in the telling. Lilly spent her year in workshops full of people. Stabby spent his surrounded by an entire clan who came to worship him. Ghostbloom stayed behind at the Guild with Vane, because where Ursa was going had no roads and he would not risk her.

So it was a boy of eight, a staff, a fur cloak, and eleven months of walking.

**The first four months were ordinary,** and the table should hear that, because the ordinariness is what makes the rest land. He worked for meals. He slept in barns and under hedges and once, memorably, in a hollow tree with a badger who did not want him there and, thanks to the Mark, eventually got over it. He got very good at being alone. He got very good at asking old people questions, because old people remember the direction a stranger walked off in twenty years ago.

Then the maps ran out.

**The Skyless Country** is what the last people out that way call it, and they say it the way you say the name of a bad neighbour. It is not a wasteland. It is worse than that, in a quiet way: it is perfectly ordinary land, hills and streams and thin woods, under a sky that is *wrong*. Cloud that never breaks. Not storm cloud. A low grey lid, day after day, week after week.

For a druid who navigates by constellations, it is a blindfold.

He lost eleven days in there. He will not talk about the eleven days.

> **Illustration slot.** Small figure with a staff on an empty grey hillside, enormous flat sky, no stars, no horizon line worth speaking of. The loneliest picture in the book.

### The cairns

What saved him was that his father had been there first.

Six days after he stopped being able to navigate, wet through and rationing badly and frightened in a way he had not admitted to himself yet, Ursa came over a rise and found a pile of stones. Just a cairn, waist high, the kind any traveller might leave. Except the top stone had a mark scratched on it, and the mark was a seven-pointed star wrapped in a leafed vine, and Ursa had been looking at that sigil on Elaria's gifts since he was seven years old.

His father had built a road of them. Not a path on the ground. A line of cairns across a country where you cannot see the sky, each one placed exactly at the edge of sight of the last, so that a person could cross the blind land one stone at a time.

Ash had built it going out. Ursa walked it going in.

It took him five weeks and he did not lose the line once. He also, and this is the detail that undoes people, **repaired eleven of them**, because a few had fallen and there was no reason to assume he would be the last person to need the road.

### The omens begin

Somewhere in the Skyless Country, Ursa started dreaming numbers.

He would wake with two of them in his head, clean and certain and meaning nothing, and then at some point in the day he would need exactly one of them, and the moment would arrive with the number already sitting in his hand like a stone he had picked up hours ago.

He thought he was going mad for about a fortnight. Then he started writing them down on waking, and found he was right every time, and stopped thinking he was going mad and started being quietly and profoundly frightened instead.

His father was a sky-reader. Under a sky he could not read, something else had started reading *for* him.

## Part Three: The Shrine

He came out of the Skyless Country into clear air and old forest, and eight days later he found the place.

**The old shrine, far east beyond the maps.** Older than the Circle. Older, probably, than anything with a name in it. A ring of standing stones half swallowed by a glade, every one of them carved with the same seven-pointed star, worn nearly smooth by a great deal of weather and a great deal of time.

And in the middle of it, a camp.

> **Illustration slot.** The glade at dusk. Standing stones, moss, low slanted light. A long-cold firepit in the middle with a stone shelf beside it. Nobody in the picture. This one should hurt a little.

### What he found

Nobody. Ursa did not find his father. That is the first thing to say plainly at the table, because the players will hope, and the hope is the point.

He found the camp his father made, and he found it in the condition of a place somebody meant to come back to.

A firepit lined with stones. A shelter of bent living branches, still holding, because a druid built it and asked the tree nicely. A stack of split wood under a stone overhang, dry, ready. A tin cup. A worn place on a flat rock exactly the shape of somebody who sat there most evenings for a long time.

Nothing was packed. Nothing was buried. Nobody had said goodbye to this place.

And on the stone shelf beside the firepit, in a small worn hollow where something round used to sit, there was nothing at all.

### How long ago

Here is the thing that changed Ursa's whole year, and it is a small thing, and he found it by doing exactly what Selarin told him to do.

*Read the ground too.*

The bent-branch shelter was still alive. Growing. A shelter like that is coaxed out of a living tree, and the tree keeps growing afterward, and the wood had closed around the bindings by an amount that Ursa, who has been a druid since he could walk and knows precisely how fast a young ash tree closes over a rope, could read like a page.

Not fifteen years.

**Three.**

Ursa's father walked east and vanished when Ursa was a baby. Everyone the boy has ever met has spoken about Ash Catchum in the past tense for his entire life. And Ash Catchum sat in this glade, on that flat rock, splitting that firewood, **three years ago**.

He was alive far, far more recently than anybody believed.

Ursa sat down in the middle of the standing stones and cried for a while, which he has not mentioned to Lilly or Stabby, and then he got up and got to work, because he is his father's son and that is apparently what they do.

### The stone

He found it under the flat rock, wrapped in oilcloth, put there on purpose by someone who wanted it to survive the weather.

A river stone, smooth as an egg, dark grey. On one face, carved carefully and well, the seven-pointed star of Elaria wrapped in its leafed vine.

**Half of it.**

The line stops. Not broken, not worn: *stopped*, mid-stroke, the way a pen stops when someone speaks your name. Ursa knows his father's hand from a bundle of notes he had read forty times by then, and this is his father's hand, and his father put the chisel down in the middle of a line and wrapped the stone and hid it under a rock and did not come back to finish it.

When Ursa closed his hand around it, the stone was warm.

It has been warm ever since.

> **Illustration slot.** Close on his hands, the half-carved stone, the unfinished line. Faint gold in the groove. No face, no background: just the object and the boy's hands.

### The night the sky came back

He stayed at the shrine eleven days, and on the eighth the cloud broke for the first time since the Skyless Country, and Ursa Catchum lay down flat on his back in the middle of a ring of ancient stones and looked up at a sky he had not seen properly in four months.

He knew immediately that something was wrong with it.

There was a hole in the sky. Not a rift: a **gap**, a place in a constellation he had known since he was small where a star was simply not, and had not been for a long time, and the dark around it had a shape.

Betelgeuse. The one in his father's notes, underlined twice.

He had grown up hearing that the stars were going out. He had heard it the way children hear that a war happened somewhere before they were born. Lying on his back at the shrine, nine years old by now, a very long way from anyone who loved him, he *looked at the missing one*, and it stopped being a story.

That is the night the staff woke up.

### The vision

He did not fall asleep so much as get taken.

The standing stones lit from the inside, all of them at once, the seven-pointed star burning in the rock like a coal under ash. And a voice that Ursa had heard exactly once before, at a glade a very long way west when he was seven years old and the whole world was still simple, said his name.

**Elaria showed him three things.**

**The first was his father, walking.** Not fleeing, not falling: *walking*, deliberately, toward a light that was going out. The vision would not show his face and Ursa understood without being told that this was on purpose. What it showed him instead was the intent, and the intent was this: **Ash Catchum went east to save the stars.** Not for glory and not for a discovery. He went because the lights were failing and somebody had to go and see why, and he was the best sky-reader alive, and so it fell to him.

Ursa has carried, since he was old enough to understand the word *disappeared*, a small ugly question he never once said out loud: *did my father choose to leave me?*

The answer is yes. And the reason is the best reason there has ever been.

**The second was the thing behind it.** A wall at the bottom of everything, and behind the wall a hunger with patience, pressing. He knew the name before the vision gave it to him, because he has been hearing it in the Circle's old verses since he was small.

**Maldrith.** The Weaver of Fractures. Sealed beyond the last wall when Elaria spent herself, and pressing on it ever since, and the stars are going out because she is winning.

**The third was a shadow that walks for her.** A figure in black on a high place with the sky dying behind it, putting out a light with its hand as calmly as a person pinching a candle. And the vision gave that one a name too, the way you hand somebody a weapon.

**Nyxthid.** The Harbinger of Decay.

Ursa came off that shrine floor with his heart going like a bird and one clean thought in his head, and it was not grief and it was not fear. It was a *task*. His father went to save the stars. Something is putting them out. It has a name, and now he has the name.

He said it out loud, alone, in a ring of standing stones, nine years old, and swore at the top of his lungs that he was going to find it.

> **DM notes, critical.** This is the FIRST time the name Nyxthid is spoken in the campaign; verified against every session document. Maldrith the players already know, from the Lore of Elaria boxes in Sessions 2, 3 and 4, so that name lands as a connection snapping shut rather than as exposition.
>
> **Nyxthid is Ash.** Ursa has just sworn to hunt his own father and will spend four sessions doing it. Elaria did not lie to him: every single thing she showed him is true. She simply did not show him that the man walking east and the shadow in black are the same person, because he was eight years old and alone and about to walk home across a country with no sky, and she needed him to survive the trip.
>

## What He Learned

That his father was not lost. His father was **interrupted**.

Those are entirely different things and Ursa has built the rest of his life on the difference. A lost man is a tragedy you learn to carry. An interrupted man is a sentence somebody stopped in the middle, and Ursa has read enough of his father's handwriting now to know that Ash Catchum does not leave sentences unfinished by choice.

And the second thing, which is quieter and which the table will feel later: he came home able to see about one second further ahead than anyone else, and he did not earn that on purpose. It came to him in a country with no sky, from a direction he cannot name, and he is not entirely sure it is a gift, and he has decided to use it anyway.

## What He Brings Back

**Reader of Omens.** After a long rest he wakes with **two d20s** already rolled, written down before the day starts, his to spend on any roll he or anyone he can see makes. He carries **both Weal and Woe** every day now instead of one or the other, and he can wait until **after the die has landed** before deciding which to apply. Five uses from a single pool, and his tides run deeper than an ordinary star-reader's: **1d6 + 2** either way.

**The Awakened Gifts.** Kneeling at his father's shrine woke the things he was already carrying. The **Staff of Waking Constellations** became a **+2**, its **Starseed** grew from 1d4 to **1d8**, and the **Amulet of Guiding Light** now reaches **every ally within 30 feet** instead of only those standing in its glow.

**Ash's Sigil-Stone.** Warm since the day he picked it up. It gives him **Aura of Vitality** once a day without a slot, and it holds **two charges** he can spend to **Ask the Day**, **Glimpse the Trail**, or **Read the Heart**. It is his father's unfinished work and he does not let anyone else hold it.

**Elemental Fury**, from the year of practice: **+5** to the damage of any of his cantrips.

**What he does not bring back:** his father. Say that out loud once. He has been walking for a year and he is closer than he was, and he does not have him.

## The Thread He Is Pulling

Three years. Not fifteen.

Somewhere between this shrine and now, three years ago, a wildfire druid stood up from a flat rock in a ring of standing stones, put down a half-finished carving, and went to do something he expected to come back from.

Ursa is going to find out what. He has a name for the thing that took his father from him, and he has said out loud that he is going to find it, and he does not go back on a thing he has said out loud either. That runs in the family.
