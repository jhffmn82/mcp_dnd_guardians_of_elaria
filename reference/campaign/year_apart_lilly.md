# Lilly Glimmergear: The Year of Questions

## The Tale

She went home first.

That surprised everyone, including Lilly. She had spent two years climbing out of the Underdark, and the moment Gearhaven gave her a year to spend, she spent the first month of it walking back down. Deepforge Hollow smelled the way it always had, of hot metal and cold stone and the faint green tang of the lantern moss. Her mother was waiting at the door of the workshop with flour on her hands, because Poots Glimmergear had never in her life managed to do two things at once without ruining one of them.

Lilly put the Essence Sphere on the kitchen table and said: tell me everything.

What followed was the least satisfying conversation of her life. Her mother told her about the day she brought it home, about the long nights fitting the brass around it, about the sigil work along the equator that took eleven tries to get right. She talked like a craftsman describing a commission. And Lilly, who had grown up on her mother's stories and could recite most of them backward, noticed the shape of the hole in this one. Poots never once said where the thing inside the brass had come from.

So Lilly went looking.

She started at Glimmerspire, in the vaulted workshops where she had been the smallest apprentice in a hundred years. Master Droven Sparkwick was older and slower and just as impossible to impress, and he made her earn every answer. She rebuilt three of her own childhood projects to his satisfaction before he would even look at the Sphere. When he finally did, he looked for a long time, and then he said something that stayed with her the whole year: *I taught your mother. I did not teach her this.*

After that she followed the trade roads. She sat at the benches of a dozen artificers, and she paid for their time in the only currency she had, which was work. She spent a season with a clockmaker in a hill town who could not cast a single spell and whose escapements were better than anything in Gearhaven. She spent six weeks with a woman who built prosthetic hands for miners and who taught Lilly more about how a thing should feel in a person's grip than any book had. She went back through Gearhaven twice and let Guildmaster Vane shout at her about tolerances until her ears rang, and she got better, and better, and then noticeably better, until the guild artificers stopped talking over her head and started asking what she thought.

That was the crafting. It filled her hands and it made her formidable. But it was not the year's real work.

The real work was the Sphere, and the Sphere would not answer.

She took it to wizards after that, which is a different animal entirely. Artificers ask what a thing does. Wizards ask what a thing *is*, and they are insufferable about it, and they were exactly what she needed. She showed it to a diviner who fainted. She showed it to a theorist who insisted it was a forgery and then wrote her four increasingly frantic letters over the following months. She learned to read three scripts she had no business reading. She filled a notebook, then another, then a third, and the third one was mostly crossings out.

The answer, when it came, was not in a library. It was in the brass.

She had taken the housing apart perhaps forty times that year, and on the forty first she stopped looking at her mother's beautiful sigil work and started looking underneath it. There were older marks under the brass. Not damage. Not tooling. Marks, deliberate and fine and utterly unlike any hand she had been trained to recognize, running along a surface that should have been the inside of a shell her mother made and was instead the outside of something else.

The brass was Glimmerspire. The brass was her mother, eleven tries and all.

The thing inside the brass was old. Not old like the Academy, which had stood four hundred years. Old like the mountain the Academy was cut into.

She sat on a workshop floor a very long way from home and understood, all at once, that her mother had not built the Essence Sphere. Her mother had found it, and recognized it for something, and spent a year of her own life building a house worthy of it. Poots Glimmergear, who could not do two things at once, had done the second best thing a person can do with a mystery: she had kept it safe and she had handed it on.

Lilly wrote to her that night. The letter was four pages and the first three were angry. She burned those and sent the fourth.

## What She Learned

Here is the part that mattered, and Lilly worked it out herself, on that floor, with her notebook open and her hands still greasy.

She had spent her whole life trying to *build* her way to Pikachu. Better cannon, better dagger, better plating, better everything, as though the Sphere were a lock and cleverness were a key. It was never a lock. It is a **gatherer**. It was made, by someone, a very long time ago, to draw in loose planar essence and hold it. That is all it has ever done. It held Pikachu because Pikachu's spark was loose and the Sphere does not know the difference between a scrap of stray fire and the soul of somebody's friend.

You cannot pick a gatherer's lock. You can only give it something to gather.

So she stopped building at it and started listening to it. That is the whole of what she learned in a year, and it is the reason she came home different.

## What She Brings Back

**Aether Channeler.** She can draw on the Sphere now, a little. Not command it: draw on it, the way you draw water from a well you did not dig. What she can pull out today is a shell of gathered motes, the **Aether Ward**, which wraps her and every friend within 30 feet in 2d8 + 5 temporary hit points. Twice, and then she needs a short rest before the Sphere will give again.

That is deliberately almost nothing compared to what the Sphere can do, and Lilly knows it. The five great discharges sleep in it: fire, water, air, earth, and shadow. Each one wakes only when that plane's Mote is brought back and set into the housing. Every rift the party closes hands her another piece of a weapon she is only beginning to understand. **The more planar energy the Sphere gathers, the more it will teach her.** For the first time in her life, Lilly's power grows by going *out* rather than by staying up all night at a bench.

**Flash of Genius.** A year of watching better crafters fail taught her the most useful thing an inventor can know, which is what failure looks like one second before it happens. When she or any friend within 30 feet is about to come up short, she can throw her whole considerable mind at the problem and add +5. Five times a day. It is not an invention. It is attention, which she now has in enormous quantities.

**The workshop year.** Her hands got good. Boomstick came out of that year, her mother's old repeating pistol rebuilt into something that is at once her weapon, her spell focus, and her arcane firearm. So did a small pile of gifts, which she laid out on the table the morning the three of them met again:

| For | What | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Puff** | Ring of Spell Storing | So her homunculus can carry a spell she has no hands left to cast. |
| **Puff** | Wand of Magic Missiles | Darts that never miss, for a companion who cannot roll well. |
| **Puff** | Pipes of Haunting | Because Puff is small and frightening things is a good trade. |
| **Herself** | Wand of Web | The battlefield control she never had. |
| **Stabby** | Wraps of Unarmed Power +2 | His fists were the only weapon he owned with no magic in them. She fixed that. |
| **Ursa** | Wand of the War Mage +2 | Every spell he throws, and every star-arrow, lands truer. |

She did not make any of it for herself first. That is worth noticing about her.

## The Thread She Is Pulling

She came home with her mother's honesty restored and her mother's authorship gone. Somebody made the Essence Sphere. Somebody made it to gather planar essence, long before there was a crisis worth gathering it for, and then it sat in the dark until a gnome with flour on her hands picked it up.

Lilly does not know who. She has decided she is going to find out.
