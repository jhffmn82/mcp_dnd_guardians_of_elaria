# Stabby Sharpblade: The Year He Went Back

## Part One: The Smallest Goblin in the Hollow

Every clan has one child who is a problem, and the Bloodfang had Stabby.

He could not do any of it. That is the part nobody outside the Hollow knows, because he has never once said it out loud. Goblin children learn the clan's arts from the time they can stand, and the arts simply did not take with him. Every stance slid off. Every drill went wrong in a way the instructors could not explain and eventually stopped trying to. He was not lazy and he was not stupid. He was *unteachable*, in a clan that had exactly one measure of a person's worth, and by the time he was five everyone had quietly agreed what he was going to be.

So he became the other thing. He became the joke. He was the loudest goblin in the Hollow, the one on the roof, the one who stole the chief's dinner on a dare and ate it in front of him. If they were going to laugh anyway he was going to decide what they laughed at, and he got extremely good at it, and it worked, in the way armor works.

And underneath it he taught himself to fight out of pure spite. Speed, cunning, and chaos, because those were the only three things nobody could take away from him. He beat a whole pack of rival goblins with a broken sword when he was small enough to hide in a barrel, and they gave him the name Sharpblade for it, and he still could not do a single one of the clan's forms.

Then he left. He told everyone it was for adventure. That was true and it was also the smaller half of the truth.

### Skitch

Two years later, the party pulled a half-dead goblin courier out of the machinery under Gearhaven, and when Skitch could talk again the first thing he said was not thank you. It was a name. *Bloodfang.* The Hollow was in trouble. The Hollow had sent him a very long way to find the only Bloodfang anybody could name who had gone out into the world and come back alive from anything.

Stabby laughed at him. That is the honest version and it belongs in the telling. He laughed, because the clan asking for him was the clan that had spent his whole childhood explaining that he was not worth teaching.

Then he stopped laughing, packed in about four minutes, and went.

### What was wrong

Bloodthorn Hollow had been going quiet for two years, and nobody there had a word for why.

It was not a war. Wars, the Bloodfang understood. This was a blight in the twisted woods, thin and patient, and it did not take the weak ones. It took the *strongest* first. The biggest hunter in the clan, then the second biggest, then down the list, and every goblin it took came back wrong or did not come back at all.

Understand what that did to them. The Bloodfang had one law, which was that the biggest goblin wins, and something in the woods was eating them in order, largest to smallest. By the time Stabby walked back in, the clan was mostly the small ones. The overlooked ones. The ones who had survived on speed and cunning and chaos, because nothing had ever considered them worth taking.

He knew every single one of them. He had been the smallest of them once.

### He Does Not Go Back On His Word

This part he did the way he does everything, which is fast and loud and extremely well.

He spent eleven weeks hunting the things the blight had made out of his clansmen. He moved the whole Hollow twice, uphill and then across the water, over furious objection, because goblins hate moving and he was right. He set traps a cleverer person would not have thought of and a braver person would not have risked. By the end of the summer the Bloodfang were not dying anymore.

But he had not fixed it. He had only outrun it.

The blight had no body to put a sword through. It had a root somewhere under the twisted woods, and everything he killed grew back thinner and further out, and Candyfang went through the corrupted things the way a knife goes through smoke: it hurt them, and the wound closed. He could keep his clan alive like this for years, standing in front of them, never sleeping.

He was nine years old, and he understood with complete clarity that he was not enough and would have to go away and become enough.

He told the whole Hollow so, standing on a stump, at volume. He said he was going, he said he was coming back, and he said that when he did he would burn the thing in the woods out of the ground, root and all. Goblins do not make promises like that, because goblins who make promises like that die. Nokk says he did not even sound worried.

He has never once gone back on a thing he said out loud. Everyone who has ever met him knows that about him, and most of them think it is a joke.

### The Fourth

Before he went, he asked Nokk a question he had been carrying since he was five.

*Why could I never learn anything you people tried to teach me?*

Nokk is old and half-blind and the last goblin alive who remembers the Hollow the way it was. She sat him down and told him the truth, which nobody had told him, because the ones who knew had agreed among themselves that it was kinder not to.

His father was the **Fourth**. The fourth leader the Bloodfang ever named, and by every account the best of them.

Nine years ago something came out of the deep woods. Nokk will not describe it well, because she was running at the time, and everyone who got a proper look at it is dead. What she knows is that the Fourth went out to meet it alone, and that he did not kill it, because it was not the kind of thing that can be killed.

He **sealed** it. He put it somewhere it could not get out of, and the only vessel to hand that night was his newborn son, and it cost him his life to do it.

The clan is named Bloodfang for that thing. Not for their own teeth. The **Red Fang** is older than the Hollow, and the Bloodfang have been the family that keeps it bound for longer than they have kept records, and every so often a bearer is born and the clan is supposed to know what to do about it.

They had forgotten what to do about it. That is the whole tragedy in one line.

**And that is why the arts never took.** There was something enormous asleep behind Stabby's ribs, pushing back against every form they tried to pour into him. He was never unteachable. He was *occupied.* Nine years of being the clan joke, and the reason was that his father loved the Hollow enough to hand his son the worst thing in the woods rather than let it loose on them.

Stabby did not say anything for a long time. Then he asked whether there was anyone left who could teach him to carry it.

Nokk told him the Bloodfang had a breathing form once, and that they had not invented it. It was taught to them, generations back, by people who came down out of the mountains with dark swords, *specifically so that a bearer could survive being one.* The clan let it die, because a goblin who spends two years learning to breathe loses every fight during those two years, and in a clan where the biggest wins nobody survives to finish.

She could not perform a single step of it. She remembered the count.

"Go and find whether they are real," she said. "And if they are, tell them the Hollow kept the count."

## Part Two: The Mountain Where Nothing Moves

They are supposed to be a story.

Everyone has heard some version. A sect past the treeline on a mountain called Kettlecrown, where the sun arrives before it arrives anywhere else in the world. They forge swords from ore that drinks dawnlight. They spend their lives hunting what crawls in from outside the world, the corrupted and the rotted and the wrong. They do not take students, they do not come when called, and most sensible people file them somewhere between the tooth fairy and the goddess.

It took him two months to find the road and another nine days to climb it. And when he finally hauled himself over the last ledge, filthy and triumphant and ready to meet the greatest swordsmen in the world, what he found was bullywugs.

### The Vigil

Frog-folk. The same wide-mouthed, damp, croaking people that every traveller's tale puts knee-deep in a swamp stealing boots. Except these ones were sitting on stone spires above the cloud line in the thin gold light, motionless, eyes half closed, doing absolutely nothing at all with tremendous authority.

And they were **enormous**. That is the thing nobody tells you about bullywugs, because almost nobody meets an old one: they never stop growing. A swamp bullywug dies young and stays the size of a person. Give one four hundred years of sitting still on a mountain and it ends up the size of a farm cart.

Stabby said a word here that has been left out of the record.

But think about it for one second longer than he did, because he came around to it eventually and so will the table. If you were going to build a school of **breathing** anywhere in the world, you would build it out of frog-folk. A bullywug is a bellows with legs. The whole body is a lung and a throat and a patient working set of muscles built for one long pull of air, and it can hold perfectly still for six hours on a warm rock and consider that time well spent.

The oldest of them is called **Grandmother Ottick**. She is roughly the size of a hay wagon, which tells you exactly how old she is, and she has been sitting on the same spire since before Bloodthorn Hollow had a name. The swords come off their forge lower down the mountain, where the young ones work the crimson ore, and the young ones are two hundred years old.

### The Test Is Stillness

They did not care that he was a goblin. That surprised him, and it is worth landing at the table, because he had a whole speech ready. When you are four centuries old and the size of a hay wagon, a goblin and a human and an elf are all much the same thing: small, brief, and in a hurry.

They cared about one thing, and they knew it about him before he opened his mouth. The oldest of them looked at him for rather a long time and said, mildly, *you are carrying something,* and Stabby discovered that he had been braced his entire life for someone to say that and had no idea what to do when they finally did.

Then they tested him, and he failed badly.

They asked him to be still. Not to sit quietly, which he could have faked. To be *still*, entirely, from the inside, in the moment before violence. Every one of them could do it in their sleep, which is the entire problem with learning anything from a bullywug: they cannot explain how, because they have never once had to try. Grandmother Ottick's complete instruction, delivered over three months, was **stop wriggling**.

They put him on top of a spire to do it, on a flat stone no wider than a door, with a very long way down on every side and nothing to hold on to. Not as a punishment. Bullywugs consider this restful.

He could not do it for a breath. Not one. And this time he knew why, because he could feel the thing behind his ribs shift every time he tried to go quiet, the way a sleeping animal shifts when the room goes silent. Stillness is the one condition under which you can hear what is inside you, and Stabby had spent nine years being the loudest goblin alive precisely so that he would never have to.

He failed for eleven weeks. He nearly left twice.

### The One Second

It broke in the third month, in the dark, with his lungs burning and his hands shaking, when he heard Master Kaelon Windstep say the thing he had said years ago that Stabby had never once managed to do.

*When the whole world is screaming, little storm, get still.*

He had always taken it as an instruction to calm down, which is why he ignored it, because he had no intention of calming down and never would. It was not that. It was smaller and far more useful. Kaelon had not asked him to become a calm person. He had asked him to be still **for one second**, at the one moment that mattered, before everything came apart.

One second, Stabby could do.

And in that one second, for the first time in his life, he got a good look at what he had been carrying. The Vigil's masters were ready for what happened next. Nobody was hurt. Afterward the oldest of them said the only thing anyone had ever said to Stabby that made the whole shape of his life click over into sense:

*The breath is not a cage. Your father built the cage. The breath is a door, and you are the only one who gets to decide which way it opens.*

Grandmother Ottick blinked at him once, slowly, which from a bullywug is a standing ovation.

### The Blade

The Vigil forge their own steel and will not forge for anyone who has not lit their breath, which is why a Nichirin sword is a graduation and not a gift. Bullywugs, it turns out, are magnificent smiths. They are unhurried, the old ones are enormously strong, and damp skin does not flinch at a forge.

The ore came out of Kettlecrown's deep cut, crimson and strange, drinking the dawn the way dry ground drinks rain. He worked the bellows for eight days of it. What came off the anvil looked disappointing, and that is the point: sheathed and cold, a Nichirin blade is dark and plain and could be any sword in any village.

Then he ignited his breathing, and the steel caught it, and the whole length burned blood-red from guard to tip.

That is what the blade is *for*. It is not a magic sword he happens to own. It is a channel. Held still by the breath and poured out through sun-forged steel, the Red Fang's strength stops being a thing that eats him from the inside and becomes a thing that comes out of his hands at whatever he is pointing at.

They taught him **the basics and nothing more**, and they were honest about it. The form takes a lifetime and he had one winter, and there was a clan under a blight that could not wait for him to become a master.

They told him one more thing on the way down, which he has not repeated to anyone. The Vigil has been busier in the last five years than in the previous fifty. Whatever is tearing holes in the world is making a great deal of work for the people who clean up after it.

## Part Three: Hero of the Hollow

He came down off Kettlecrown in deep winter and went home.

### The Purge

The root of the blight was under the twisted woods and it had grown while he was gone, and this time he brought the right tool and the right lungs.

**Cleansing Edge is not a metaphor.** Corruption cannot heal from a Nichirin wound and cannot hide from the person holding one. He lit his breath, and the Fang's strength came up through him and out along the blade instead of tearing around inside him looking for a way out, and what had been eating his clan largest to smallest could not grow back the parts he cut away. He was patient about it in a way he has never been patient about anything.

It ended before spring.

Understand what that made him in that place. The Bloodfang had watched their biggest and best taken one after another for two years, and the thing that finally walked out of the twisted woods with the blight dead behind it was the runt. The clan joke. The one they had all quietly agreed was not worth teaching, who turned out to have been carrying their oldest enemy since the night he was born because his father chose the Hollow over his own life.

He came back for them when nobody in the world would have blamed him for staying gone. He kept a promise he had shouted off a stump. He burned the thing out of the ground, root and all, exactly as he said he would.

There is not a goblin in the Hollow who will hear a word against him now. He is their hero, in the plain and permanent way a clan decides such things, and it embarrasses him enormously.

### The Thing That Actually Unlocked It

Here is what surprised him.

He came home a decent student of the first form and no more. What made him something else was **teaching it**.

He taught Nokk's count to every goblin left in the Hollow, which was nearly all of them, because the blight had taken the big ones and the small ones had inherited a clan. He taught it badly at first. To explain a thing you have to understand it, and to explain it to a room of impatient goblins you have to understand it completely, and somewhere in that winter, standing in front of forty clansmen clapping out an old woman's rhythm, Stabby stopped performing the form and started *having* it.

The Vigil gave him the basics. His clan gave him the rest, by making him say it out loud a hundred times until it was true.

Most of them will never finish it. Some will. And the next Bloodfang child born carrying something will not spend nine years being told they are unteachable, because there is a nine year old in the Hollow now who knows exactly what that feels like and has written the count down.

### They Offered Him the Seat

Leader of the Bloodfang, at nine, the smallest goblin in the Hollow, by the only law they had ever recognized. His father's seat. The Fifth.

He said not yet.

Not no. He was careful about that, and Nokk noticed, and she is the only one who did. He turned it down because he was leaving in the spring and a leader who leaves is worse than none. But he did not say never, and when they pushed him he grinned at them with every tooth he owns and told them to hold it for him, because one day he is coming back to take it properly.

Then he gave them Candyfang. His candy-striped blade, the one Faelan gave him in the deep winter, the one he carried through every fight of his life until the Nichirin came. He put it in the Hollow's keeping and named it the clan's heirloom, and by their new law the heirloom is carried by whoever the clan would follow.

Which is why it is still on his belt. It is not his anymore. It is theirs, and he is only wearing it.

He left in the spring, made Gearhaven with ten minutes to spare, and he will tell you that was luck.

## What He Learned

That he was never the joke, and never unteachable, and never too small. He was carrying something nobody had told him about, and the moment somebody finally did, the whole shape of his life turned out to have been a different story all along.

He is not calmer. Do not let anyone at the table think he came back calmer. He is exactly as much of a menace as he ever was. He has simply learned where to put the stillness, and one second turns out to be enough to change everything that happens after it.

# The Subclass: Warrior of Beast Breathing

He has set down the Way of the Kensei. Agile Parry and Kensei's Shot are gone. What replaces them is his own clan's lost form, taught to the Bloodfang generations ago by the Sunward Vigil for exactly one purpose: so that whoever carries the Red Fang can survive carrying it.

**The idea behind it.** Beast Breathing is not a rage, which is what everyone assumes. It is the opposite. You take everything hot and fast and screaming in you, and you hold it perfectly still, and only then do you let it out through the blade. All of the ferocity, none of the flailing. For Stabby that is not a metaphor about temperament. There is something behind his ribs and the breath is what decides which way the door opens.

### What he gets

**Igniting the Breath** *(free, when he rolls initiative, 1 Focus)*
The one second, at the one moment it matters. His breath catches and burns until the fight ends. While it burns:
- every hit with the katana deals **+2 Force damage**
- his Speed increases by **10 feet** (45 on his own feet, 55 with the Sash, **65 ignited**)
- he has **advantage on Dexterity saving throws**

The blade takes the colour of the form, so the Nichirin burns blood-red while lit. Because Uncanny Metabolism refunds all his Focus when he rolls initiative once a day, turn one of the day's first fight costs him nothing.

**First Form: Devour** *(bonus action, 1 Focus; replaces Flurry of Blows)*
Move up to **15 feet without provoking**, then one katana strike for **3d8 + 9 Force** instead of normal damage. **If it drops the target he gets the Focus back**, so a Devour that finishes something is free and he can do it again next turn. The name is not decoration. This is the Fang's hunger, aimed on purpose.

**Beast Sense** *(passive)*
Advantage on Survival to track, on Perception using smell or hearing, and on **Intimidation**. He is a scout now, which he was not before, and he is unexpectedly frightening.

**Monk Weapon** *(passive)*
The katana counts as a Monk weapon for him, so his Martial Arts die applies and he keeps his bonus-action unarmed strike. With Lilly's wraps that punch is +10 for 1d8+7 and can deal Force.

### How his turn actually runs

- **Initiative:** ignite the breath. Uncanny Metabolism refills Focus to 7 and heals him. Speed is 65.
- **Action:** two katana swings at **+10** for **1d10+7** each, plus 2 Force apiece while lit. Stunning Strike on the first hit against something dangerous.
- **Bonus action:** **First Form: Devour**, a third strike at 3d8+9 Force with 15 feet of free movement in front of it. Or just punch, at +10 for 1d8+7.
- **Reaction:** Deflect Attacks, reducing a hit by **1d10+12** and throwing it back if it reaches zero.
- **New at 7:** **Evasion.** When something explodes near him he takes nothing on a success and half on a failure.

### Why this build matters from here on

**Cleansing Edge.** Once a turn, when he hits a fiend, an undead, a shadow creature, or anything empowered by planar corruption, that creature **cannot regain hit points** and **cannot hide from him in darkness** until his next turn.

Everything that comes through a rift is on that list. He did not pick the subclass that hits hardest. He picked the one built for the specific enemy this campaign has been pointing at since Session 1, and he picked it because a mountain full of very old frog-folk who have spent centuries cleaning up planar corruption looked at a nine year old goblin and recognised what he was carrying.

## The Thread He Is Pulling

The idol was in his pack the whole year. Up Kettlecrown and back down again.

He never showed it to the Vigil, which is strange, because the Vigil are the world's foremost experts on exactly the sort of thing it probably is, and he had months of evenings in which to mention it. He never showed it to Nokk either. It sat at the bottom of his bag through every month of the count, cold the way it has been cold since the day it appeared there after Wraithpine and nobody would admit to putting it there.

Twice that winter he woke up holding it. He does not remember getting it out.

He has decided this is not worth mentioning to anyone, which is the single most Stabby decision he made all year.
